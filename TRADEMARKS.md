# Trademark Policy

The Apache-2.0 licence covers the **code** in this repository. It does not grant
rights to the **names and marks** used to identify the project. This file states
what those rights are, so that forking is unambiguous.

## Marks

"Visp", "Visp Kit", "Visp Hyper Agent", "Visp Memory", "Visp Dev", and the Visp
logo are marks of the Visp project.

## What you may do without asking

- State truthfully that your software uses, integrates with, extends, or is
  compatible with Visp.
- Use the marks in prose, documentation, articles, talks, and comparisons,
  including critical ones.
- Keep the marks in unmodified copies you redistribute — the licence expects
  attribution to be preserved.
- Use them in a package name that clearly signals a third-party relationship,
  such as `visp-plugin-yourthing`.

## What requires permission

- Naming a **modified** distribution in a way that implies it is the official
  one, for example publishing a fork as `visp-kit`.
- Using the marks in your product name, company name, domain, or logo in a way a
  reasonable person could read as endorsement.
- Implying certification, partnership, or official status.

## If you fork and modify

Rename the artifact. A fork carrying the original package name is the one case
that genuinely confuses users, because the name is how they decide what to
trust — and this project's entire claim is about trustworthy verdicts. Keep the
copyright notices and the `NOTICE` file; change the name.

## Assurance claims

Do not describe output as a Visp verdict, Visp assurance, or Visp-verified
unless it was produced by unmodified Visp components. A modified engine may
reach different conclusions, and the value of the claim depends on which code
made it.

## Asking

Open an issue describing the intended use. Uses that are plainly honest and
non-confusing are normally fine; the reason for asking is the small set of cases
where they are not.
